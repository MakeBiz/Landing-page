Comparing supplier quotes: criteria and a comparison table

Four suppliers sent four quotes in four different formats, and your buyer has to work out which one is actually cheaper. Here is a table template, the order to compare in, and how an AI agent builds the table from emails and files.

Compare supplier quotes line by line and on one basis: unit price excluding VAT in AED at a recorded exchange rate, delivery, lead time and stock, payment terms, minimum order, warranty and quote validity. Set the weights for each criterion before you open the quotes. Compare line items, not quote totals

A quote total is almost never comparable as it stands. One supplier priced nine items out of ten, another quoted per box instead of per piece, a third included delivery and a fourth did not. Put the totals side by side and the winner is whoever left something out.

So each row in the table is a line item from your request, and each column is a supplier. Before you open any replies, lock the item list, the quantities and the base unit: piece, kilo, metre. Every quote then drops into the same grid, and gaps show up straight away.

Nine columns cover almost any purchase

For every item and every supplier, the same fields get filled in. This template is a good place to start:

A word on currency and VAT: the standard UAE VAT rate is 5%, and if one quote includes it and another does not, that gap can easily outweigh the difference between two close offers. We covered four traps like this in our news piece on where money leaks when comparing suppliers.

Set the weights before you open the quotes

Weights invented after the fact quietly bend towards the supplier someone already likes. So write them into the purchase record up front. A hypothetical set for consumables: price 50, lead time 20, payment terms 15, track record 15. For an urgent order you raise the lead time weight, for a large batch payment terms and minimum order matter more.

Scoring is simple: the supplier with the lowest price gets 10 points on price, the others get proportionally less. Lead time works the same way. The total is the sum of scores multiplied by the weights.

Track record comes from history, not gut feeling: how often a supplier missed a date, changed the price after quoting or went silent. You only have that data if purchasing runs in a CRM rather than in personal spreadsheets. How to set up the request process itself is in our guide to procurement automation.

The agent builds the table from emails and files

Done by hand, it looks like this: the buyer opens every email, hunts for the price in a PDF, types it into Excel, converts boxes to pieces and hopes nothing was missed. The procurement agent does the same job by the rules you set:

A worked example: four quotes for consumables

A hypothetical company asks four suppliers to quote on ten items. The first has the lowest total, but two items are missing and VAT is not stated. The second quotes per box of 12. The third offers made to order with a long lead time. The fourth includes delivery to the warehouse. Once everything is on one basis, the first quote goes back for clarification, and the real choice is between the second and the fourth, settled by the lead time weight agreed in advance.

Questions and answers

How many suppliers should we compare per purchase?

There is no fixed rule: for routine purchases three to five is common, for a large one-off order you ask more. What matters is that everyone gets the same specification and the same reply deadline.

What if a supplier does not state VAT?

Do not guess, ask in writing. Until they reply, mark the quote as incomplete, otherwise a 5% gap can make the choice for you.

Can we compare quotes in Excel?

Yes, if there is one template for all purchases and the table is kept with the decision. Problems start when every buyer has their own file and the price history leaves with the employee.

How does the agent get supplier quotes?

From email and messengers connected to the CRM. It reads PDFs, Excel files and email text, maps the figures to request line items and records the source of each value.
