Bitrix24 smart processes: what they are and when you need them

Once your sales pipeline fills with cards that are not sales, the portal starts confusing everyone. Bitrix24 smart processes give those processes a place of their own. Here is how they work and when they are worth it.

A Bitrix24 smart process is a custom CRM entity with its own stages, fields, permissions and automation rules, built for purchase requests, contracts, service requests and anything else that does not fit into a deal. You need one when a process has its own path, its own people and needs its own report. A smart process is a CRM entity of its own, next to deals

Bitrix24 CRM comes with standard entities: leads, deals, contacts, companies, quotes and invoices. They describe a sale well. But a company runs other processes too: a department's purchase request, a contract going through sign-off, a client's service request after the sale. Put those into deals and the sales pipeline fills with cards that are not sales, while revenue reports stop adding up.

A smart process fixes that. An administrator creates a new entity with its own name, and it gets everything a deal has:

  • its own kanban stages and, if needed, several pipelines
  • its own fields: amounts, dates, lists, files, links to employees
  • links to contacts, companies, deals and other smart processes
  • automation rules and triggers on stages, just like the sales pipeline
  • separate access rights: who can see, create and move items
  • documents generated from templates right inside the item

Whether smart processes are available, and how many, depends on the Bitrix24 plan, so we check the plan before quoting.

Deals, smart processes, tasks and workflows do different jobs

The usual problem in a portal is not a lack of tools but mixing them up. This table helps decide where a process belongs.

ToolWhat it is forTell-tale sign
DealA sale to a clientThere is an amount, a probability and sales stages
Smart processA recurring process with its own stages: purchasing, contracts, serviceMany similar items, each with its own path and people
TaskA one-off assignmentOne person, one result, no stages
Workflow (business process)A chain of steps with conditions and approvalsBranching logic on top of any entity

Smart processes and workflows are not rivals. Stages and items live in the smart process, and a branching approval runs as a workflow at the right stage. Simple actions such as creating a task, changing the owner or sending a notification are handled by automation rules.

Purchase requests travel from request to payment in one item

Take a hypothetical trading company: departments send purchase requests in a group chat, the finance director approves them in private messages, and a month later nobody remembers who signed off what. A "Purchase request" smart process puts it all in one item.

  1. New. An employee fills in what is needed, how many and by when
  2. Approval. A rule sends an approval task to the line manager, and anything above a threshold goes to the finance director
  3. Quotes. The buyer requests supplier quotes and attaches them to the item
  4. Ordered. A supplier is chosen and the item is linked to its company record
  5. Received and closed. Delivery is confirmed, then payment

Collecting and comparing quotes can go to the procurement AI agent: it sends requests to suppliers and puts the quotes side by side. Where money leaks at this step is covered in our news on comparing suppliers.

Contracts get their own stages and deadlines

A contract is linked to a deal and a company, but it outlives the sale. In a "Contracts" smart process it has stages of its own: draft, review, client sign-off, signed, active, expiring. Fields hold the number, the amount in AED, the start date and the end date.

Automation rules do the rest: a set number of days before the end date the owner gets a task to talk to the client about renewal, and the document itself is generated from a template using the item's data. A rep opens the deal and sees the linked contract in a tab, with no digging through folders or email.

Service requests show what happens after the sale

After a sale the client needs installation, maintenance or an answer to a complaint. If that runs through chats, the manager cannot see how many requests are open or how many are stuck. A "Service request" smart process is linked to the company and the deal, and a rep creates one from the client record in a click.

The stages are simple: received, assigned, in progress, waiting for client, done. A delayed rule reminds the engineer if a request sits too long on one stage, and the manager sees a kanban of every open request. If requests arrive on WhatsApp, the channel connects to Bitrix24 open channels, see the WhatsApp in Bitrix24 page.

A smart process pays off when three signs are present

Not every process deserves its own entity. A smart process makes sense when:

  • the process has its own stages, and dozens of similar items go through them
  • different people or departments take part, and it matters who is on which step
  • you need a report on it: how many items, at which stage, how long in progress

If it is really one or two fields on a deal ("delivery needed: yes or no"), a separate entity only makes the portal harder to use. Questions and answers

How is a smart process different from a deal?

A deal describes a sale with an amount and stages. A smart process is a separate entity for another process, such as purchasing or contracts, with its own stages, fields and permissions, and it keeps the sales pipeline clean.

Can a smart process be linked to deals and companies?

Yes. A smart process links to contacts, companies, deals and other smart processes, and linked items show up in each other's tabs.

Do automation rules and workflows work in smart processes?

Yes. Automation rules and triggers are set on smart process stages, and workflows handle approvals with branching logic.

Are smart processes available on every Bitrix24 plan?

Availability and the number of smart processes depend on the plan. We check it before quoting so the process does not hit a limit after go-live.
