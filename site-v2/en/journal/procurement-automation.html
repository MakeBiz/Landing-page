Procurement automation: from purchase request to supplier choice

In a small or mid-sized company, purchasing usually lives in email, WhatsApp and Excel: requests arrive in a group chat, RFQs go out by hand and quotes end up in a spreadsheet nobody can find later. Here is which stages a system can take over and what stays with your buyer.

Procurement automation covers the path from purchase request to supplier choice: a request in the CRM, RFQs sent from a specification, quotes collected, compared on price, lead time and terms, then approved. The MakeBiz Technologies procurement AI agent handles the RFQs, follow-ups and quote consolidation, while the decision stays with a person. One agent costs AED 5,000 one-off to implement, and support is 10% of the implementation fee per month, so AED 500.

Purchasing runs in six stages, and each has an owner

Before you automate anything, break the process into stages. Most companies have six, even if nobody calls them that. The table shows who runs each stage once it is automated and where the data ends up.

StageWhat happensWho does it after automationWhere the data lives
1. RequestSomeone states what they need, how many and by whenThe employee fills in a CRM formRequest record
2. ApprovalA manager confirms the need and the budgetA CRM automation rule routes it by amountRequest record
3. RFQsThe specification goes to several suppliersThe AI agent sends RFQs and chases repliesEmails and chats in the CRM
4. Quote collectionReplies arrive as PDFs, Excel files, emails and WhatsApp messagesThe AI agent reads them and maps them to line itemsDocuments in the record
5. Comparison and choicePrices, lead times and terms are put side by sideThe agent builds the table, the buyer decidesComparison table in the CRM

Stages three to five carry most of the manual work, so that is where the agent goes. The rest is ordinary CRM automation.

Requests belong in the CRM, not in a group chat

Automation starts with a request form, not with AI. A voice note in a group chat tells no system what to buy. In Bitrix24 a purchase request works well as a separate smart process with its own stages: new, awaiting approval, RFQ sent, comparison, ordered, closed. See how these are built on our Bitrix24 development page.

Required fields:

  • item or catalogue reference, quantity and unit of measure
  • required date and delivery address: warehouse, office or site
  • budget estimate and the cost centre it is charged to
  • attachments: photo, drawing or specification, if there is one

Without these fields a request does not move on. A CRM rule sends it to the line manager, and above a threshold you set, to the finance director too. No AI needed here.

The agent takes over RFQs and supplier follow-ups

After approval comes the part buyers like least: sending the specification, chasing silent suppliers and tracking who got what. The procurement agent does this itself: it takes suppliers for the category from your database, sends the RFQ by email or messenger and follows up with anyone who has not replied.

Every message is saved in the request record, so history stays when people leave. If your suppliers work on WhatsApp, the channel is connected to the CRM through the official platform, see our messengers in Bitrix24 page. The agent does not promise volumes and does not negotiate: if a supplier asks something outside the specification, the question goes to the buyer.

Quotes go into one table on one basis

Replies come as scanned PDFs, long Excel price lists or a price typed into an email. The agent reads each document and maps it to the request line items: unit price, currency, VAT included or on top, lead time, stock, payment terms, warranty. What this kind of extraction can do and where it stops is covered in our guide to invoice and document recognition.

What matters in a comparison is not speed but a common basis. Prices are converted to one currency at a recorded rate and date, all amounts are shown excluding VAT, and the tax (the standard UAE rate is 5%) sits in its own column. The four places where money leaks during consolidation are covered in our news piece on comparing suppliers, and a table template with every column is in the guide to comparing supplier quotes.

Lines that do not add up are flagged, so the buyer reviews those instead of the whole table.

The decision and the accountability stay with a person

The agent prepares the choice, it does not make it. Some criteria are written down nowhere: the supplier who saved you on an urgent order, a cheap newcomer with no track record, a discount agreed in a meeting. The buyer sees the table and the agent's reasoning, then decides.

These stay with people:

  • negotiating price and terms, especially with key suppliers
  • choosing a new supplier for an important category
  • exceptions: urgent orders, a single source, unusual payment terms
  • signing the purchase order or contract

The line between an AI agent and a CRM automation rule is explained on our AI in Bitrix24 page. In short: a rule executes a condition, an agent works with text and documents, and a person owns the decision.

Questions and answers

Can we automate procurement without changing our CRM?

Yes, as long as the CRM can hold a separate request process and connect to email. We usually build it in Bitrix24 as a smart process, and the agent connects to it and to your messengers.

Which purchases should we automate first?

Frequent, repeat purchases with a clear specification and several suppliers: consumables, packaging, standard services. One-off and strategic purchases come later, once the rules are settled.

Does the agent choose the supplier?

No. It sends RFQs, collects quotes, builds the comparison table and gives a recommendation with its reasoning. The decision, the negotiation and the signature stay with your buyer.

How much does procurement automation cost?

One agent on the Start package is AED 5,000 one-off, with support at 10% of the implementation fee per month. CRM setup and accounting integration are quoted separately, and the estimate is fixed before work starts.

Where is supplier and price data stored?

In your CRM: requests, emails, documents and comparison tables sit in the records. Access and data stay with your company, and we work under a separate user account.
