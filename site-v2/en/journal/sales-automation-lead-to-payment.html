Sales automation: from lead to payment

Deals are not lost on the big steps but in the gaps between them. Here is how to connect enquiries, the CRM, an AI agent and your accounting system so a deal reaches payment without anyone retyping data.

Lead-to-payment sales automation connects your web forms and WhatsApp, the CRM deal, the invoice in your accounting system and the payment status, with no manual data entry in between. MakeBiz Technologies · 11 November 2026 · 7 min read

Sales automation closes the gaps between stages

Sales teams rarely lose money on one big step, they lose it in the gaps: a website enquiry sits unanswered for half an hour, a rep forgets to follow up after sending a quote, the invoice goes out two days after the client said yes, and nobody marks the payment in the CRM. Together these gaps stretch the deal and hand the client to whoever replied faster.

Automating the path from lead to payment removes those gaps. Data moves from stage to stage without anyone retyping it, and every step has an owner: a CRM automation rule, an AI agent or a sales rep. The sales manager can see where a deal is stuck before the client walks away.

CRM automation handles the rules

Anything that reads "if X, then Y" is a job for Bitrix24 automation rules: cheap, predictable, no AI needed.

  • create a lead from a CRM form and keep the UTM tags
  • route enquiries between reps by queue or product line
  • set a call-back task if the client has not replied
  • move the stage when a quote is sent or an invoice is issued
  • alert the manager if a deal has not moved for longer than a set period

Automation rules are part of our standard Bitrix24 setup, which starts at AED 10,000 with go-live from 14 days. Connecting WhatsApp to open channels costs AED 500, with the provider subscription on top, see WhatsApp and messengers.

An AI agent takes the steps that need language and judgement

A rule cannot give a client a proper answer or notice that a deal has stalled because the client is unsure about price. That calls for an agent. The AI sales manager replies to the client when the rep goes quiet and keeps an eye on the pipeline: it flags deals that have stopped moving and tells the manager where help is needed. The agent connects to your CRM and messengers, so it works with the same data as your team.

We explain the difference between an agent and an automation rule in our news piece AI agent or CRM automation. The short version: rules go to automation, conversations and judgement calls go to the agent, and decisions on discounts and terms stay with the rep. The Start package with one agent is AED 5,000 one-off, and maintenance is 10% of the implementation cost per month.

Roll it out step by step

  1. Map the process as it is: where enquiries come from, who answers, where deals wait the longest
  2. Set up the pipeline and stages with clear exit criteria
  3. Connect the channels: website CRM forms, WhatsApp, telephony
  4. Add automation rules: routing, tasks, alerts
  5. Connect the CRM to accounting for invoices and payments
  6. Add the AI agent for first replies and pipeline control
  7. Train the team, and after a month check where deals still wait

To measure the effect, record three numbers from last month before go-live: time to first reply, time from the client saying yes to the invoice going out, and the share of deals with no activity for over a week. A month after launch compare them to see which step worked.

Questions and answers

Where should sales automation start?

With a map of the process as it is: where enquiries come from, who answers and where deals wait the longest. Then you set up the pipeline, channels and automation rules, and only after that add an AI agent.

What is the difference between CRM automation and an AI agent?

An automation rule runs "if X, then Y": create a lead, set a task, move a stage. An agent understands text and context: it replies to clients, spots stalled deals and tells the manager about them.

Can I issue invoices with 5% VAT straight from Bitrix24?

The invoice is triggered from the deal, but your accounting system produces it: Zoho Books, QuickBooks or Odoo. Your finance team sets up tax, and the integration passes deal data across and returns the payment status.
