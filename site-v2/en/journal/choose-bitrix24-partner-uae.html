How to choose a Bitrix24 partner in the UAE

You pick a partner for years, not weeks: they build your CRM, hold admin access to the portal and pick up the phone when something breaks. Here is what to check before you sign.

Check a Bitrix24 partner in the UAE on 8 points: a listing in the partner directory on bitrix24.ae, a contract with a UAE legal entity and invoices in dirhams, a written estimate with an hourly rate, access registered to your company, support after launch, the team's working language, real case studies and a training plan. For reference, a standard CRM setup at MakeBiz Technologies starts at AED 10,000 and extra work is AED 200 an hour.

Eight checks before you sign

Most of these take one call and one email. If a partner is vague on two or three of them, stop before the deposit, not after it.

Partner status is checked in the vendor directory

Official Bitrix24 partners in the region are listed in the partner directory on bitrix24.ae. Ask the contractor for the link to their listing and open it yourself. Going through a partner also makes it easier to buy and renew licences and to switch plans without losing data.

Status alone does not guarantee a good setup, but it filters out resellers who pass your project on to someone else. MakeBiz Technologies is an official Bitrix24 partner in the UAE, and the scope of our work is on the Bitrix24 implementation page.

Contract, invoices and estimate are fixed before work starts

For a UAE company it is simpler to work with a contractor that has a legal entity in the Emirates: a contract with a UAE company, invoices in dirhams, payment to a corporate account. Expenses are easier to book and disputes are easier to settle.

The estimate should show what the total is made of: standard setup, hourly custom work, integrations, training. Our standard CRM setup starts at AED 10,000, work beyond the standard is AED 200 an hour, connecting WhatsApp is AED 500 and an accounting integration starts at AED 10,000. All prices are on the pricing page. The Bitrix24 licence is paid separately, and we cover how to pick one in the guide on plans for 10, 25 or 50 users.

Ask separately how changes during the project are priced. The normal practice is that a new task is estimated in hours first, you approve the estimate in writing, and only then does the work start. If a partner is happy to make changes "as we go and settle up later", you will only learn the total at the end, when it is too late to argue about it.

Access and data stay with your company

The portal, the WhatsApp number, telephony and integration accounts should all be registered to your company. The contractor works under a separate admin account that you can switch off in one click.

  • the portal owner is your employee, not the contractor
  • passwords to services stay with you, the contractor joins by invitation
  • access is closed when the work ends, and the contract says so

If a partner offers to "register everything under us, it is faster", you risk losing your client history when you change contractors. The same questions apply to an AI vendor, and we listed them in the news piece on 7 questions to ask before signing.

Support after launch matters more than a fast start

Teams ask for the most changes in the first two or three months: new fields, reports, automation rules, permissions. Ask up front how support works: an hour package, response time, who exactly answers. Our Bitrix24 support packages are AED 1,250, 2,300 and 4,200 a month for 5, 10 and 20 hours.

If your portal is already live and you are switching contractors, start with a review of the current setup. What that covers is on the Bitrix24 audit page.

Case studies, language and training are checked on a call

Ask to see one project similar to yours: the task, what was set up, what changed. A good partner will also tell you what did not work the first time. Our projects with the task and the result are in our case studies.

Dubai teams often work in English and Russian at the same time, so check which language the partner trains in and writes guides in. Training works best by role: sales reps need the pipeline and tasks, managers need reports, admins need permissions and automation. How we run it is on the team training page.

A worked example: two quotes for the same job

Take a hypothetical trading company in Dubai with 15 sales reps that gets two quotes. The first says "CRM setup for AED 4,000" with no scope. The second lists a standard setup at AED 10,000 plus 15 hours for the website integration and reports at AED 200, AED 13,000 in total, with stages and a launch date.

The first looks cheaper until the change requests start: each one is billed separately and there is no contract with a scope of work. Three months later the totals are close, but the company has neither an estimate nor a clear portal owner. Compare the scope and terms from the table above, not the bottom line.

If you already have both quotes, put them side by side against the eight checks and ask each contractor to fill the empty cells in writing. The choice usually becomes obvious, and price stops being the only argument.

Questions and answers

How do I check that a company is an official Bitrix24 partner?

Look it up in the partner directory on bitrix24.ae. Ask the contractor for the link to their listing and open it yourself.

How much does a Bitrix24 implementation cost in the UAE?

At MakeBiz Technologies a standard CRM setup starts at AED 10,000 and work beyond the standard is AED 200 an hour. The Bitrix24 licence is separate: cloud plans start at AED 180 a month billed annually.

Can I switch partners if my portal is already live?

Yes. The portal and the data belong to your company, so the new partner joins by invitation and the old one loses access. Start with a review of the current setup.

Who should the Bitrix24 portal be registered to?

To your company and one of your employees. The contractor works under a separate account that you can switch off.

What should the contract with a partner include?

The scope of work and hourly rate, stage deadlines, support terms, who owns the access and how it is closed when the work ends.
