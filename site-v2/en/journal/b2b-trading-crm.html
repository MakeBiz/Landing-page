CRM for a B2B trading company in the UAE: long deals and accounting

In a trading company a deal runs for weeks, the client has several people involved, and the invoice comes out of the accounting system. A simple enquiry, call, payment pipeline does not cope with that.

A B2B trading company in the UAE needs a CRM where each client company has several contacts, deals move through stages from enquiry to payment, and quotes are built from a catalog. In Bitrix24 this is covered by standard setup from MakeBiz Technologies starting at AED 10,000 and going live from 14 days. Integration with Zoho Books, QuickBooks or Odoo starts at AED 10,000 on top, with the estimate fixed before we start.

A long B2B deal needs its own stages

In retail a customer decides within a day. In B2B trading, weeks pass between the first enquiry and payment: clarifying the spec, sending a quote, agreeing the price, sometimes a tender, then the contract, the invoice and the delivery. Squeeze all of that into three stages and deals sit in "Negotiation" for weeks while nobody can tell where an order is really stuck.

Build stages around the buyer's decisions, and give each one an entry criterion and an owner. A typical setup for a trading company:

StageWhat the CRM recordsWhat to automate
EnquiryCompany, contact, channel, product and volumeLead from email, form or WhatsApp, task for the rep
QualificationVolume, timing, decision maker, budgetRequired fields before moving on
Quote sentQuote built from the catalog, version, validity dateReminder if the client goes quiet
ApprovalDiscount, payment and delivery terms, who approvedManager sign-off for discounts above a threshold
Contract and invoiceContract, AED invoice with 5% VAT, client entityInvoice from accounting, status in the deal
Delivery and paymentDelivery date, part payments, balance duePayment follow-up task on the due date

Repeat orders from existing clients work well in a separate pipeline: they have a different cycle and different tasks. Mix deal types in one pipeline and conversion reports stop making sense, see our guide on why CRM reports lie.

In B2B the client is a company with several contacts

One buyer usually means several people: a procurement officer asking for prices, an engineer checking the spec, a finance team that pays and a director who signs. If the CRM holds a single contact, your rep is missing half the conversation.

In Bitrix24 every contact is linked to the company, and the deal shows who plays which role. Add the fields that matter in the UAE: the client's legal entity, its TRN for invoices, and whether it sits in a free zone or on the mainland. Running several of your own entities in one portal is covered in our news piece on free zone and mainland in one Bitrix24.

Email belongs in the record too. Connect shared and personal mailboxes, and messages attach to the company and the deal.

Quotes come from the catalog, not from Word

When reps put quotes together by hand, prices, SKUs and terms drift apart. In the CRM a quote is built from the product catalog: line items, AED prices, discount, 5% VAT on its own line, validity date. Every version is saved in the deal, so a month later you can see exactly what the client received.

For more involved approvals (a special price request, a credit limit check, a contract with custom terms) smart processes do the job. When you need them is explained in our guide to Bitrix24 smart processes, and custom work is on the Bitrix24 development page.

Accounting holds the money, the CRM shows it

A common mistake in trading companies is tracking invoices and payments in both the CRM and the accounting system. Within a quarter the numbers diverge and nobody knows which to trust. The working model is simpler: the invoice is issued in accounting, and the CRM receives its number, amount and payment status.

Between Bitrix24 and Zoho Books, QuickBooks or Odoo you would typically sync:

  • the client list with company details
  • products, prices and stock, if your accounting system holds them
  • invoices and their status: issued, part paid, paid
  • receivables for each client

Repeat orders and overdue payments show up on the client record

Regular clients in trading reorder again and again, and the CRM makes sure you do not miss the moment. An automation rule creates a task when a client has gone longer than usual without ordering, and an overdue invoice triggers a reminder with the balance pulled from accounting.

You can, but a trading company is safer issuing invoices in its accounting system and passing the number, amount and payment status to the deal. That way CRM and accounting figures stay in line.
