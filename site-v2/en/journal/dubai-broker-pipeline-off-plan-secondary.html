Sales pipeline stages for Dubai brokers: off-plan and secondary

Most Dubai agencies sell two different products: units under construction from developers, and ready homes on the resale market. Squeeze them into one pipeline and they get in each other's way, because each deal has different parties, different paperwork and a different point where the commission lands.

A Dubai brokerage needs two pipelines of 7-8 stages each. Off-plan ends when the developer registers the contract in the DLD interim register (Oqood), which has to happen within 90 days of signing; secondary ends with the DLD transfer, where the fee is 2% from the seller and 2% from the buyer. Setting up both pipelines with fields and automation is part of a Bitrix24 implementation from AED 10,000.

MakeBiz Technologies · 17 November 2026 · 7 min read

Off-plan and secondary deals need separate pipelines

In an off-plan deal the other party is the developer. The broker takes the buyer from choosing a project to the booking and the sale and purchase agreement, and from there the developer's sales team does much of the work. On the secondary market the other party is an owner, often with their own agent, and the broker runs the deal end to end: negotiation, deposit, developer clearance and transfer.

In one shared pipeline the stages turn into vague labels like "in progress", and the manager can no longer tell where a deal is. That is why in Bitrix24 we build a pipeline per deal type, as described on our CRM for Dubai real estate agencies page, and portal leads are routed into the right one from the start.

The off-plan pipeline ends with Oqood registration

The DLD page on registering an initial sale says the service is used by the developer to register units sold off-plan in the interim register, through the Oqood developer portal. The contract has to be registered within 90 days of signing. The same page lists the fee as 2% of the sale value from the seller and 2% from the buyer.

That gives the CRM two rules. First, the SPA signing date is mandatory at stage 6, and an automation rule gives the broker a check task on day 60 to confirm the registration has gone through. Second, the developer pays the agency under the agency's own agreement with that developer, so the commission rate and payout condition live on the project record, not in the broker's head. Under Dubai Law No. 8 of 2007, buyer payments for off-plan units go into the project escrow account, as the DLD FAQ explains, and that line belongs in the message template you send to buyers.

The secondary pipeline turns on the deposit and the developer NOC

On resale, the slowest stages are outside the broker's control. According to the DLD page on property sale registration, properties in freehold areas need an electronic no-objection certificate (e-NOC) from the developer through the Dubai REST app. The fee is the same 2% from each side, plus the trustee centre fee: AED 4,000 plus VAT when the sale value is AED 500,000 or more, AED 2,000 plus VAT below that.

So the secondary pipeline gets its own "Developer clearance" stage with the request date and a rule that reminds the broker to chase the status after 3 working days. If the buyer is taking a mortgage, a bank approval stage is added, and the deal cannot move to transfer without it.

Automation and manager control at every stage

Stages only help if something happens at them without the manager chasing people. The minimum set of rules for both pipelines:

  • a new lead with no first reply after 15 minutes alerts the manager, as covered in our piece on CRM for brokers and portal leads
  • after each viewing, the broker gets a task to log the client's feedback the same day
  • a deal stuck for 7 days at stages 3 to 5 creates a task and shows up in the report
  • WhatsApp message templates at the booking and deposit stages; how the channel is connected is on the messaging page
  • at "Commission received" the amount flows into the per-broker report

A report across both pipelines shows where off-plan buyers drop out, how long resale deals wait for an e-NOC, and which broker turns deposits into transfers. Turning those stages into a revenue forecast is covered in our guide to sales forecasting from CRM data.

Questions and answers

Why does a broker need a separate off-plan pipeline?

Off-plan has a different counterparty, the developer, and the deal ends with the contract registered in Oqood. Mixed with resale, the stages blur and the manager cannot see where each deal stands.

Who registers an off-plan sale with DLD?

According to the DLD service page, the developer registers the initial sale in the interim register through the Oqood portal. The contract must be registered within 90 days of signing.

What is the DLD fee on a ready property sale?

The DLD sale registration page lists 2% of the sale value from the seller and 2% from the buyer, plus a trustee centre fee of AED 4,000 plus VAT from AED 500,000 or AED 2,000 plus VAT below. Checked 17 November 2026.

Can rentals run in the same CRM?

Yes. Leasing becomes a third pipeline with its own stages: enquiry, viewings, tenancy contract, payment, move-in. Properties and clients are shared across all pipelines.

On a new project the pipelines are included in setup from AED 10,000. In a portal that is already live, splitting them usually takes a few hours at AED 200 an hour.
