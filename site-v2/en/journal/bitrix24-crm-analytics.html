Why CRM reports lie and how to fix them

A sales director opens the pipeline report and does not believe it: conversion jumps around and revenue does not match the accounts. The report is rarely the problem. The data going into it usually is.

CRM reports go wrong for four reasons: duplicate clients, empty key fields, vague pipeline stages and no rules on who fills in what and when. The fix starts with a portal audit, which MakeBiz Technologies bills at AED 200 an hour with the estimate fixed before we start. Once the data is clean, BI dashboards start at AED 300 a month, with the first 14 days free.

A report shows exactly what your team typed in

CRM reports do not count sales, they count records of sales. If a rep creates the same client twice, skips the lead source or closes a deal a month after payment, the report counts exactly that. So "the report is broken" almost always turns out to be a problem inside deal records, not in the widget settings.

Duplicates inflate the database and split client history

A duplicate appears when a client messages on WhatsApp and later calls from another number, or when two reps add the same company under slightly different names. The report sees two clients and two histories. In reality it is one buyer who already has two different quotes.

Bitrix24 can find and merge duplicates by phone, email and company name. A one-off clean-up will not last unless you close the door: a check when a record is created, one phone format, and no new company without a search. Who may merge or delete records is an access rights question, covered in our news piece on CRM access rights.

Empty fields turn analytics into guesswork

An empty field is not a zero, it simply drops out of the report. If only some deals have a loss reason, your "why we lose clients" report draws conclusions for everyone from that subset.

Making every field mandatory does not help: reps will type anything just to save the deal. What works:

  • keep the 5 to 7 fields you actually decide on
  • require each one only at the stage where the information appears: amount at the quote stage, loss reason at closing
  • fill in automatically whatever can come from a form, a call or an invoice
  • drop fields nobody has opened in a quarter

Every stage has to mean the same thing to everyone

For one rep "Negotiation" is the first call, for another it is an agreed contract. The stage report then shows habits, not your sales process. Give each stage an entry criterion you can check: "Quote sent" means a quote is attached, "Invoice issued" means the invoice exists in the CRM or your accounting system.

Keep as many stages as your client has real decisions, usually 5 to 8. If nobody can describe a stage in one sentence, merge it with the next. Restrict stage skipping, or stage-to-stage conversion stops meaning anything.

Questions and answers

Why does my Bitrix24 conversion report show odd numbers?

Usually deals are created without going through leads, or the same client exists several times. Reports count records, so start with how leads come in and with duplicates.

How do I remove duplicates in Bitrix24?

Built-in duplicate search finds matches by phone, email and company name, and you can merge them. To stop them coming back, use one phone format and check for an existing record before creating a new one.

How many fields should be mandatory?

Usually the 5 to 7 fields you make decisions on. Require them at the stage where the information appears and fill the rest automatically.
