WhatsApp Business API or WhatsApp Web in your CRM

In the UAE customers message you on WhatsApp, so it is one of the first channels a company connects to its CRM. There are two ways to do it: the official WhatsApp platform or a WhatsApp Web connector. Here is how they differ.

For a UAE sales team the official WhatsApp Business Platform (API) is the safer choice: one number for the whole team, conversations inside the client record, templates and clear limits. A WhatsApp Web connector relies on third-party software, and the WhatsApp terms for the Business App prohibit apps that interact with the service without WhatsApp's written consent. The two options are different by design

The official platform is a business channel: your company number is verified by Meta and connected to the CRM through a provider. WhatsApp Web in a CRM is a workaround: a program or browser extension reads chats from the app on a phone and copies them into the portal.

WhatsApp's own terms settle the question

The WhatsApp Terms for WhatsApp Business App (effective 23 September 2026, section 5 "Restrictions") say a company must not develop or use any applications that interact with the Business App services without WhatsApp's prior written consent, and must not scrape or extract data from them. The same terms describe the app as designed for small to medium businesses.

A WhatsApp Web connector is exactly that kind of third-party app. It works fine until it does not. If the terms are breached the account can be restricted, and with the number the company loses the channel its clients know it by. That is a sales risk, not a matter of taste.

The 24-hour window and templates come with the API

According to Meta's pricing documentation, a message from a customer opens a 24-hour customer service window. Inside it your rep replies in free text, outside it you can only send a template. Templates are approved by Meta in advance: a meeting reminder, an order status, an invoice.

For a CRM this means response speed has a price. In Bitrix24 we set up response time control: if a message has no reply for 15 minutes, the manager is notified while the window is still open. How an agent can sort incoming leads is covered in our news piece on the AI agent in WhatsApp.

Templates fall into categories: marketing, utility and authentication, while ordinary replies inside the window count as service messages. The category sets the price of a message, so name templates in the CRM in a way that lets a rep see at a glance which one is promotional and which one is for handling an order.

Broadcast limits grow with quality

Meta's messaging limits page defines the limit as the number of unique WhatsApp users a business can message outside a customer service window in a moving 24-hour period. A newly created business portfolio starts at 250. Completing one of the scaling paths raises it to 2,000.

After that the limit rises automatically if messages across all your numbers and templates are high quality and you use at least half of your current limit over 7 days. That is why blasting a list of people who never opted in hurts twice: complaints lower your quality rating, and the limit goes down with it.

What each option costs

The official channel has three budget lines:

  • connection and setup in Bitrix24: AED 500 one-off with us, covering the number, open channels, routing and deal fields
  • the API provider subscription: at the provider's rates
  • Meta message fees: they depend on the market and the message category, and since 1 October 2026 service messages above 1,000 a month per number are charged too, see our news on WhatsApp Business pricing in the UAE

WhatsApp Web looks free, but the bill arrives later: time spent reconnecting, chats that never reached the CRM and the risk of losing the number. The full scope is on the WhatsApp and messengers in Bitrix24 page, and all prices are on the pricing page.

To see your own number in advance, export last month's outgoing messages from the portal and split them into templates and live replies from reps. That is quicker than guessing from the rate card.

How to move from WhatsApp Web to the API

  1. Decide which number becomes the company number and export the chats you need from the app
  2. Pick an API provider and complete Meta's business verification
  3. Set up open channels: queue, working hours with UAE weekends, auto-reply
  4. Link chats to leads and deals and add response time control
  5. Get 3 to 5 templates approved: first message, reminder, invoice, status
  6. Tell clients if the number changes and switch off the old connector

The longest waits are usually business verification and template approval. That is why the old connector is switched off only once the new channel is live and the team is used to it, so sales do not stop during the move.

A worked example: an agency with 8 sales reps

A hypothetical Dubai agency runs WhatsApp Web in its CRM: every rep works from their own phone and some chats never reach the portal. After moving to the API the agency has one number, chats are routed in turn, and the manager sees each rep's response time. One-off cost: AED 500 for the channel plus a few hours on automation and templates at AED 200 an hour.
