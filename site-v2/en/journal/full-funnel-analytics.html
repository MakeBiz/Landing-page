End-to-end analytics answers one question: which channel brings in money

Your ad account sees clicks and leads. Your CRM sees deals. Your accounting system sees payments. Each is accurate on its own, yet none can say how much revenue your Google Ads budget returned. End-to-end analytics joins them into one chain, so every payment has a source.

Without it, budgets are set on cost per lead. A channel with cheap leads looks like a winner even if those leads never pay, and a channel with expensive leads gets paused although it brings the large deals. End-to-end analytics swaps that for cost per paying customer and return on ad spend by source.

Tracking rules come before the first campaign

The most common reason for gaps in the report: ads went live before anyone agreed on tagging. A month later the CRM is full of deals with the source "website" and no campaign behind them.

  • One UTM convention. utm_source for the platform, utm_medium for the traffic type, utm_campaign for the campaign. Names in lowercase Latin letters, spelled the same way in every ad account
  • Click IDs. Google Ads auto-tagging adds gclid, Meta adds fbclid. Store them in the deal so you can send the payment back to the ad platform later
  • Hidden form fields. A Bitrix24 CRM form reads the tags from the page URL and writes them into the lead, so sales reps type nothing
  • Dedicated entry points. A WhatsApp link or number per campaign, and a separate phone number per source for calls

Revenue comes from accounting, not from the deal stage

A deal marked "Won" means the rep closed it, not that the money is in the bank: the client may pay in part, late or not at all. So the last link comes from accounting. The invoice is raised from the deal, and the payment status flows back to the CRM on its own.

Setting it up is a step-by-step job

  1. List every source: ad accounts, website, WhatsApp, phone, partners, repeat business
  2. Sign off the UTM convention and campaign naming, and share it with everyone who launches ads
  3. Configure CRM forms and open channels so tags land in the lead without the rep touching them
  4. Make the source field mandatory, with a fixed list of values for deals created by hand
  5. Connect the CRM to accounting so payments flow back into the deal
  6. Pull spend from the ad platforms into BI and match it with payments by campaign
  7. After 2 to 4 weeks, reconcile the report with the ad accounts and finance

Check the quality of older deals first: duplicates and empty fields damage a report more than missing tags, see our guide on why CRM reports lie. You can also order a stand-alone Bitrix24 audit.

The dashboard shows cost per payment, not cost per lead

The result is one table by source and a few charts. Core metrics:

  • spend per channel and number of leads
  • deals and payments from those leads
  • cost per lead and cost per paying customer
  • revenue and return on ad spend by channel
  • average time from lead to payment

The last one matters for long B2B deals: if clients pay two months after the first enquiry, last month's campaigns have not shown results yet. Questions and answers

What is end-to-end analytics in plain terms?

It is a report in which every payment has an ad source. Data from the ad accounts, the CRM and accounting is linked, so you can see how much money each channel brought in.

Can I do end-to-end analytics without a CRM?

Only partly, because without a CRM you cannot see which leads became deals and payments. The minimum is an ad account, a CRM with a source on every deal and payments from accounting.

How do I track WhatsApp enquiries and phone calls?

Use separate WhatsApp links or numbers per campaign and separate phone numbers per source. The enquiry then reaches the CRM already tagged.
