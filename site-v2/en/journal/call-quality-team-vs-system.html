Call quality control: QA team, software or both

Sooner or later every company with a sales team has to decide who listens to the calls: a QA specialist, call speech analytics software, or both. The answer depends on your call volume and on what you actually want to find out.

A QA specialist listens to a sample and is good at unpacking individual calls, software scores 100% of calls and spots patterns, and in practice the combination works: the software scores every call, a person reviews the flagged ones and coaches the reps. Sampling and full coverage answer different questions

Sampling answers the question "how did this rep handle these calls". Full coverage answers "what is happening across every call the team makes". They are different jobs, and mixing them up leads to the wrong conclusions.

Say calls break down at the same stage in a noticeable share of conversations. If a QA specialist listens to a handful of calls per rep each week, they may spot the pattern or they may not: it all depends on which calls landed in the sample. Software that looks at every call shows the share of those calls for each rep. We walked through one such breaking point in our news piece on where sales teams lose clients inside the call.

Do the maths in call minutes

To compare the options, work out your call volume in minutes: number of reps, times calls per day, times average call length, times working days. Take a hypothetical team: 6 reps, 20 calls a day each, 3 minutes on average, 22 working days. That is 2,640 calls and 7,920 minutes a month, or 132 hours of recordings.

No one person can listen to 132 hours a month and still do anything else, and scoring takes time on top of listening. That is why a QA team always works with a sample. For example, 5 calls per rep per week gives you 120 calls a month out of 2,640, about 4.5% of conversations and roughly 6 hours of pure listening.

For software, that volume fits the 10,000-minute plan at AED 1,700 a month. Under 5,000 minutes the AED 1,000 plan is enough, and larger teams with 30,000 minutes or more start from AED 4,000. All plans are on the pricing page.

To keep the comparison fair, the cost of a QA specialist is more than a salary: add the manager's time spent checking their scores, and onboarding a replacement if they leave. Software costs are more predictable, because the subscription depends on minutes, not people.

You still need a QA person with software in place

Software does not replace someone who understands your product and your clients. It will not judge whether a rep picked the right moment for a discount on an unusual deal, and it will not have the conversation with a rep who is burning out. A person is needed for three things:

  • setting and refining the criteria: what a good call means in your business
  • reviewing disputed and high-stakes calls: a large deal, a complaint, a lost client
  • mentoring: the one-to-one, practice, and a check a week later

How to run those reviews is covered in our guide on sales call analysis. We train managers and QA staff to work with the reports in our team training sessions.

Software takes the routine and surfaces patterns

Call speech analytics transcribes every conversation, scores it against the stages of your script, collects the top client questions and objections, tracks each rep over time and suggests who needs coaching. The call summary and score appear in the deal record, next to the chat history.

The big change is that nobody spends time hunting for problem calls any more. The QA person opens the report, sees where scores drop and listens to exactly those calls. If you only have two reps and a light call load, software may be overkill and a sample will do.

Full coverage has another benefit: scores are comparable across reps and months. When every call is judged by the same criteria, the "I was marked harder than my colleague" argument goes away, and coaching is about numbers rather than impressions.

The combined setup runs on a simple cycle

  1. Agree the scoring criteria with the head of sales and write them up for your script
  2. The software scores every call and updates the report
  3. Once a week the QA specialist takes the flagged calls and reviews them
  4. The sales manager runs short one-to-ones with reps, one skill at a time
  5. Once a month the criteria are checked against reality and the wording is refined

Calls are only part of the client conversation. WhatsApp follow-up and pipeline control are handled by the AI sales manager, and call recordings need to land in the CRM: what to check with your telephony provider is in our news piece on telephony for a CRM in the UAE.

A worked example: a team of 6 reps

A hypothetical Abu Dhabi company has one QA specialist who listens to about 5 calls per rep each week. Once the software is connected, they stop searching for problem calls by hand and spend the same hours reviewing flagged calls and coaching. The sales manager sees every rep's score by stage, not just the reviewer's impressions. After a quarter the company decides whether it needs a second QA person based on data, not on how busy everyone feels.

Questions and answers

Should I hire a QA specialist or use call speech analytics?

It depends on call volume. With a light call load, a sample reviewed by a person is enough. With hundreds of calls a day, software scores all of them and a person reviews the flagged ones and coaches reps.

How much does call speech analytics cost?

5,000 minutes cost AED 1,000 a month, 10,000 minutes AED 1,700, and 30,000 minutes or more from AED 4,000. Prices exclude VAT.

How do I estimate my monthly call minutes?

Multiply the number of reps by calls per day, average call length and working days. For a more accurate figure, export last month's data from your telephony or CRM.

Can I drop manual call listening completely?

We would not recommend it. Software finds the problem calls, but the criteria, disputed cases and coaching stay with a person.

Can reps see their own scores?

The score and call summary appear in the deal record. Who sees which reports is set by CRM permissions: usually a rep sees their own calls and the manager sees the whole team.
