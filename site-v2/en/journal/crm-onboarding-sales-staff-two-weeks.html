Onboarding sales staff to CRM in two weeks

In the first weeks, a new sales rep works the way the people at the next desk show them. If the CRM feels optional during that time, clients end up in a personal WhatsApp and a notebook, and moving them back later is harder than training it right from day one.

A new sales rep can be working in the CRM within 10 working days: two days of role-based sessions on your own portal, three days with a mentor on real clients, three days solo with a daily review, and two days for error review and sign-off. At MakeBiz Technologies, corporate Bitrix24 training costs AED 200 an hour, introductory training is part of a standard setup from AED 10,000, and new hires can be trained within a support plan from AED 1,250 a month.

The onboarding plan fits into 10 working days

Two weeks is enough when a new hire learns on your own portal, not a demo: your stages, your fields, your real clients. We split onboarding into four blocks, and each one ends with a check you can see in the CRM itself.

The first two days run on the company portal, and the session recording stays with you. The next hire starts from the recording, and live hours go to questions. That way you do not retrain from scratch with every new hire.

The CRM sales policy is written before the first session

New reps learn faster when the rules are written down instead of passed on by whoever sits next to them. A sales team CRM policy fits on two or three pages and answers the questions a new hire asks in week one:

  • when a deal is created and which fields are required
  • what each stage means and which event moves a deal into it
  • how to reply on WhatsApp: only through the line connected to the CRM
  • how to call: through company telephony, not a personal number
  • which next activity follows every contact, and by when

In Dubai we write the policy in English and Russian so the whole mixed team reads the same rules. If WhatsApp is not yet connected to Bitrix24, start there; details are on the Bitrix24 messengers page.

New hires are trained by role and language

Reps, managers and admins need different things. A rep works with deals, activities, chats and invoices. A manager reads the pipeline, reports and targets, and learns to run a meeting from the CRM. An admin handles users, permissions, fields and automation rules. Put everyone in one session and half the time goes to someone else's role.

Language works the same way: Dubai sales teams often work in two or three languages, and a mixed group means some people are listening to a translation. We split groups by role and language, online or at your Dubai office. The programme and format are on the Bitrix24 training page. Access rights are set before day one so the new rep sees their own clients and nothing else, as covered in our news on CRM access rights.

Progress is checked in CRM data, not in a quiz

A quiz on the interface shows that someone remembers the buttons, not that they work in the system. In week two, the manager checks three things right in the portal:

  • whether every open deal of the new rep has a dated next activity
  • whether the fields needed for an invoice are filled: the client legal name and TRN
  • whether client chats run through the CRM, or the records are empty while clients message the rep directly

These checks fit into one report or dashboard, so the manager sees them in a minute. Why empty fields and loosely defined stages later break your reports is covered in the guide on why CRM reports lie. Field reps also get the mobile app walkthrough from the guide to the Bitrix24 mobile app for field sales.

Three mistakes that send new hires back to a notebook

First, training before the portal is set up. People learn one set of stages, a month later the stages change, and they stop trusting the system. Second, no mentor on days 3 to 5, so the first real deals are handled from memory and mistakes surface a month later. Third, a manager who works outside the CRM, asks for status in a chat and wants a spreadsheet report. The new rep quickly learns the CRM is just for show. If the team is also learning to work with AI agents, the role split and error reviews are similar; see the guide to training your team to work with AI agents.

Illustrative example: four new reps in a month

Picture a Dubai company growing its sales team from 8 to 12 people: two new hires work in English, two in Russian. The policy already exists in both languages. On days one and two, each pair gets a role-based session in its own language, about 4 hours per group, 8 hours in total. From day three, each new rep has a mentor. In week two, the team lead checks a daily report on deals without a next activity and on empty fields. Sign-off on day ten happens on real deals. The next hire, two months later, starts from the session recording, and questions are handled within the support plan hours.

Questions and answers

How long does it take a new rep to start working in the CRM?

In a portal that is already set up, about 10 working days: two days of sessions, a week with a mentor and daily review, then error review and sign-off on real deals.

How much does Bitrix24 training for a sales team cost?

Corporate training is AED 200 an hour. Introductory training is part of a standard setup from AED 10,000, and new hires can be trained within a support plan from AED 1,250 a month.

What language is the training in?

English or Russian. We split groups by role and language, and the CRM policy comes in both languages.

How do you know a new rep is really working in the CRM?

From portal data: every open deal has a next activity, required fields are filled, and client chats run through the CRM. All three fit into one report.

Do sessions have to be repeated for every new hire?

No. The recording of the introductory session stays with the company, the next hire starts from it, and live hours go to questions and a review of their deals.
