BI implementation: stages, timeline and roles

BI pays off when management makes decisions from it every day. Here is how to get from questions to working dashboards, and who owns what in the project.

BI implementation runs in five stages: management questions, data sources, data model and clean-up, dashboards, and adoption. A BI project starts with management questions, not charts

The classic mistake is to build a good-looking dashboard first and look for an audience later. A month on, nobody opens it, because it answers no real business question. Start the other way round: the owner writes down 10 to 15 questions they want answered every day or every week.

  • how much money came in this month and how much is still in the pipeline
  • which rep is behind plan, and why
  • which sources bring clients who actually pay
  • where deals get stuck the longest
  • which clients have stopped buying

Each question becomes a metric with a precise definition. "Revenue" has to mean the same thing to sales and finance: paid invoices or won deals, gross or net of 5% VAT. Until that is agreed, every chart will be disputed.

Your data sources set the scope of work

The more systems you connect, the more work there is and the higher the risk of mismatches. So sources go in one at a time, starting with the main one.

  • CRM. Deals, leads, reps, tasks. In MakeBiz BI, Bitrix24 data and Bitrix24 telephony are included in the subscription
  • Accounting. Invoices and payments from Zoho Books, QuickBooks or Odoo, connected on request for an extra fee
  • Ads and web analytics. Spend and clicks for end-to-end analytics, also on request for an extra fee
  • External telephony. If calls run through a separate provider rather than Bitrix24
  • Spreadsheets. Plans and lists that live nowhere else. Move them into the CRM, or the report depends on whoever last edited the file

If accounting and the CRM are not yet linked, start with the integration: the invoice is raised from the deal, the payment flows back, and BI gets one chain instead of two. Talk about BI

The data model matters more than the visuals

The data model is the set of rules that links tables: which deal belongs to which client, which invoice to which deal, which rep owns what. Get the model wrong and the neatest chart shows the wrong number.

This is where old CRM problems surface: duplicate companies, deals with no amount, reps who left but still appear in reports, amounts in different currencies. Everything is brought to AED and to one list of reps and business lines. We cover these errors in our guide on why CRM reports lie. If nobody has reviewed the portal in a while, start with a Bitrix24 audit, and if the pipeline does not match how you sell, you need CRM setup from AED 10,000.

The project has four roles

  • Project owner. The owner or MD who signs off questions and metrics, then actually uses the dashboard
  • Data owner. Someone inside the company who knows where everything lives and enforces CRM data rules
  • Key users. Head of sales, marketing, finance: they test the screens against their own work
  • Contractor. MakeBiz connects the sources, builds the model and dashboards, and keeps them running

Access and data stay with you, the contract is with MakeBiz Technologies FZE LLC and invoices are in AED. A BI project stalls when there is no data owner: nobody fixes CRM errors, and the dashboard slowly loses trust.
