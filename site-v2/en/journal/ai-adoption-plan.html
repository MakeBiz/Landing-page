AI adoption plan for a company: the first 90 days

AI projects rarely fail because of the technology. They stall on messy data, unclear access rights and nobody owning the result. This plan for your first quarter deals with those issues in order.

The first 90 days of AI adoption break down into three stages: preparing the process, data and access; piloting one agent; and deciding whether to scale. A one-agent pilot with MakeBiz Technologies costs AED 5,000 plus AED 500 a month for support, so our part of the quarter's budget stays within AED 6,500. You can start with a free 30 to 40 minute demo on your own process.

The quarter splits into three 30-day stages

Ninety days is a framework, not a promised deadline. Each stage can run shorter or longer depending on the state of your CRM and data. What matters is that every stage has a clear output, and the next one does not start until the previous one is done.

Choose the process by money, not by hype

Your first agent belongs where the work repeats every day and where dropping it costs money. Four questions help you choose:

  • how many times a day or week does the task come up
  • what does a mistake or delay cost: a lost lead, an overpaid supplier, a candidate who joined a competitor
  • is there data the agent can rely on: the CRM, a price list, templates, chat history
  • can the result be measured with one simple number: response time, share of unanswered leads, calls scored

For a sales team we covered the order of choice in our news piece on which agent to start with in sales. If sales already run smoothly, see our guide to AI in finance, procurement and hiring, which applies the same criteria to other departments.

Name the owner before you start

Every agent needs an owner inside the company. Not an IT person, but whoever is accountable for the process: the head of sales, the procurement lead, the HR manager. They decide what the agent tells clients, where its permissions end and when a human steps in. Without that owner a pilot turns into an experiment nobody remembers a month later.

Two more roles matter. A CRM administrator opens access and looks after the fields. The managing director makes the call at the end of the quarter, based on the numbers. A missing owner is one of the reasons AI agent projects stall, so we pin these roles down in week one.

Data and access are prepared in month one

An agent works with whatever data it is given. If your CRM is full of duplicates, empty fields and stages nobody uses, the agent will repeat that mess faster than people do. So the first month goes on preparation:

  1. Review the pipeline and fields in the CRM. If nobody has looked at the portal in a while, start with a Bitrix24 audit
  2. Build a knowledge base: price list, payment and delivery terms, answers to common questions, document templates
  3. Connect channels the official way. For WhatsApp that means the Business Platform; we connect it to Bitrix24 for AED 500, details on the messaging page
  4. Give the agent its own user account with the right permissions instead of running it under an employee's login
  5. Record baseline numbers so you have something to compare against

Access and data stay with your company. If you have no CRM yet, that comes first: a standard Bitrix24 setup starts at AED 10,000 with go-live from 14 days.

The pilot runs alongside the team, not instead of it

In month two the agent goes live. For the first weeks it works in parallel with people. The AI sales manager, for example, only replies when a rep has gone quiet, and the call agent scores conversations that a manager would otherwise only spot-check. Your team sees how the agent makes decisions, and trust builds on evidence.

Every week the process owner and our team review the agent's mistakes: where it answered wrongly, where data was missing, where it should have called a human straight away. Those reviews are the real work of a pilot. The rules that come out of them go into the knowledge base, and the agent stops repeating the same slips.

Day 90 ends with one of three decisions

At the end of the quarter the managing director compares the numbers and chooses:

  • scale: add agents in neighbouring departments under the Business package, AED 10,000 with shared data and a dashboard
  • tune: the agent works but the numbers fell short, so another month goes on rules and data
  • stop: the process was the wrong pick, the agent is switched off, and data and access stay with you

A worked example: a hypothetical Dubai service company with 12 staff loses enquiries that arrive on WhatsApp after 6 pm. In month one it cleans up the pipeline and builds a bank of answers, in month two it launches the AI sales manager, and in month three it sees how many evening enquiries got a reply and turned into deals. Questions and answers

Where should a company start with AI?

Pick one process that repeats every day and costs money when it breaks, and name the person who owns it. Data and access are then prepared for that specific process.

How much does an AI agent pilot cost?

Implementing one agent with MakeBiz Technologies costs AED 5,000, with support at AED 500 a month. Over the first quarter our share comes to no more than AED 6,500, excluding VAT and licences.

Who should be responsible for an AI agent?

The process owner: the head of sales, the procurement lead or the HR manager. They decide what the agent says and where its limits are, while the CRM administrator handles access and fields.

Do I need a CRM to deploy an AI agent?

Our agents connect to the CRM and messaging channels, so without a system holding clients and deals they have nothing to work from. If you have no CRM, a standard Bitrix24 setup starts at AED 10,000.

What if the pilot does not deliver?

Find out why: the data, the rules or the choice of process. The agent can be tuned or switched off, and your data and access stay with your company either way.
