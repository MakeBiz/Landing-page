What Is an AI Agent, and How Is It Different From a Chatbot

A chatbot and an AI agent look alike from the outside, both answer in a text window. In practice they are two different tools, and mixing them up costs companies time and money. A bot answers a question and forgets it right away. An agent picks up a task and carries it through to a result, on its own

The difference comes down to three things: role, memory, and access to systems. A bot has no memory, every conversation starts from a blank page, so it never gets past a single reply. An agent has a role (say, handling inbound requests), memory of the client and prior steps, and access to working systems: CRM, email, telephony, spreadsheets. It does not just answer, it starts a process, carries it through step by step, makes decisions along the way, and reports on the result

In practice one agent cannot cover everything, it would choke on the load. So a complex task gets split into roles and chained together. The clearest example is a sales team: one agent takes the inquiry and qualifies the lead, a second creates the deal in the CRM and fills out the card, a third drafts a reply and a commercial proposal, a fourth assigns the manager a task with a deadline, a fifth tracks overdue items and sends reminders. An orchestrator passes work down the chain like an assembly line, where each agent owns its own step and no one has to hold the whole picture in their head

What this means in process numbers. An inquiry stops sitting in an inbox overnight, the first reply reaches the client in seconds, not an hour later once they have already gone to a competitor. Data lands in the CRM automatically, with no manual re-entry, so no lost contacts and no forgotten commitments. The manager is freed from routine work and focuses on deals that actually need a human. Where part of the inbound flow used to quietly slip through, now every single one of them gets logged and worked

It is important not to sell this as magic. An agent only works as well as the data is clean and the process behind it is clear. If the CRM is a mess, half the fields are empty and there is no defined process, the agent will just multiply that mess faster and add false confidence to bad data. That is why a proper rollout almost always starts not with the agent, but with a process audit and getting the data in order

How to tell what you actually need. If the task comes down to answering common questions, a plain bot is enough, do not overpay for an agent. But if you need a system that runs the process itself, from inquiry to closed deal, and owns the result, that is a job for an agent or a team of agents. That is the question to start with, not the choice of model or a flashy demo
