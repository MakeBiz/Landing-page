Where distributors lose money

Four typical gaps while sales, the warehouse and accounting live in separate spreadsheets

Only the warehouse knows the stock

A manager promises a dealer a batch that is already reserved for another client or still in transit. Stock lives in the warehouse spreadsheet or the accounting system, and sales find out about the shortage on shipping day

Price lists scattered across files

Separate price lists for retail, wholesale and dealers, plus one in dollars and one in dirhams. A manager opens the wrong version and a client gets a price they are not entitled to

Only accounting sees dealer debt

Goods ship to a client who is already over the credit limit or whose cheque has bounced. Sales learn about it when the money is already tied up in stock at the dealer

Orders in a personal WhatsApp

Dealers send orders as a message, a voice note or a photo of a list to a manager's phone. The manager is on leave, the order waits, and the history of agreements leaves with the employee

What we build in Bitrix24

Catalog and tiered prices

Products with SKUs, barcodes and units of measure. Retail, wholesale and dealers each have their own price type in the catalog, the deal picks up the price for the client's tier, and a discount above the allowed level goes for approval

Price lists in AED and USD

Each product price is stored in its own currency and converted into the deal currency at the rate set in the CRM. The dirham is pegged to the dollar, so that rate is set once. Euro and other rates are updated by hand or with an app from the Bitrix24 Market

Warehouses and reservations

Stock for every warehouse: free zone, mainland, goods in transit. Products are reserved as soon as a manager adds them to a deal and written off when the deal closes, and if stock is short the deal cannot be closed. The storekeeper receives goods from a phone, scanning barcodes with the camera

Dealer record

Price tier, emirate, licence and TRN, credit limit, payment terms, account manager and sales plan. An order that takes a dealer over the limit does not go to shipping without management approval

PDC register

If clients pay with post-dated cheques, every cheque is in the CRM: number, bank, date, amount and status. Accounting gets a reminder to deposit the cheque on time, and a bounced cheque is seen by the manager and management at once

WhatsApp orders into deals

Dealers write to the company number connected through the official API, and the order lands in the client record. The manager builds the deal from the catalog and sends the quote and invoice from a template straight from the CRM

If stock and invoices are kept in an accounting system (Zoho Books, QuickBooks, Odoo or 1C), we connect it to Bitrix24 through the API: stock levels and dealer balances come into the CRM, and a deal turns into an invoice without retyping

Currencies, VAT and free zones

UAE rules worth building into the CRM from day one, so invoices and prices do not have to be redone later

  • You can sell in dollars, but a tax invoice must also show the total and the VAT amount in dirhams, at the UAE Central Bank rate on the date of supply. We build the invoice template with both currencies from the start
  • A tax invoice is issued within 14 days of the date of supply: a shipped deal without an invoice is flagged
  • Exports outside the GCC are zero-rated for VAT if the goods leave the UAE within 90 days and the export documents are kept. In an export deal the CRM keeps reminding until the declaration and the bill of lading are attached
  • Goods from a free zone warehouse sold to a mainland client go through customs clearance: usually 5% duty on the CIF value plus VAT. The deal shows which warehouse ships the order, so these costs go into the price before the invoice
  • From 2027 B2B invoices in the UAE become electronic, and clean client details in the CRM are needed now: more on the VAT and e-invoicing page
